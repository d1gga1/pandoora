Shipping stand panels to the UK and Ireland

Same language, same customer, two completely different shipments: Ireland is in the EU, Great Britain is not. And the ATA Carnet everyone asks about is usually the wrong instrument.

Two markets, two completely different paperwork situations

This is the part that catches people out, and it is worth being blunt about: since Brexit, Great Britain and Ireland are not the same shipment, even though they are the same language and often the same customer.

If you are an Irish contractor, the practical conclusion is short and good news: there is nothing special to arrange. It is the same procedure as any EU delivery, and asking for the direct Cherbourg–Rosslare type crossing rather than the UK land bridge keeps it that way — no British customs on a shipment that never enters Britain.

The ATA Carnet question — and why it is usually the wrong answer

Almost every British contractor who contacts us mentions an ATA Carnet. It is a sensible instinct and, for panels you are buying, it is the wrong instrument.

Temporary Admission and the ATA Carnet exist for goods that enter the UK temporarily and leave again in the same state — exhibition and trade-fair displays are explicitly on the list of qualifying goods, relief runs up to 24 months, and duty and import VAT are relieved for that window. The condition is the one that matters: the goods must be re-exported unaltered. Fail to discharge the procedure properly and the duty and VAT become payable, with penalties on top.

So the rule divides cleanly on one question — does the material stay?

  • You are buying the panels and keeping them in the UK for your own stock, to reuse across shows. That is a permanent import: a normal import declaration, duty and import VAT as applicable, VAT reclaimable if you are registered. A carnet is not appropriate and using one would leave you unable to discharge it.
  • Material goes over for one show and comes straight back to the Continent afterwards. That is the carnet and Temporary Admission case.

In our trade the first case is the normal one, because a panel that is bought is a panel that gets reused — which is the entire economics of buying rather than hiring.

We are panel manufacturers, not customs agents. The commodity code, the duty rate for your specific consignment and who acts as importer of record are questions for your freight forwarder or customs broker, and they should be settled before the order rather than at the border. What we do is supply the commercial documentation the declaration needs, correctly and on time.

What this means for lead times

Great Britain is the longest route we serve and the only one with a customs step, so it is the one that needs the most notice. Not because anything is difficult, but because two things have to happen in sequence rather than in parallel.

Freeze the sizes earlier. On a GB build-up we ask for dimensions confirmed roughly three weeks out rather than the week of the show. A change after the consignment is documented means re-documenting it, and that is where days go.

Name the importer of record at order stage, not at dispatch. It is one line on the paperwork and it is the single most common cause of a consignment sitting still.

In exchange, what arrives arrives finished: cut to size, edges done, cut-outs machined, connectors in the box, assembled dry on the stand with no saw on site. Standard sizes are held in stock and we normally dispatch within 7 days of order confirmation — the customs step sits after that, not instead of it.

Ireland, by contrast, plans like any EU delivery: no customs step, and the only date that matters is your build-up slot.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need an ATA Carnet to bring stand panels into the UK?

Not if you are buying them and keeping them. A carnet and Temporary Admission are for goods that enter the UK temporarily and are re-exported in the same state — relief runs up to 24 months and the goods must leave unaltered. Panels you buy for your own stock are a permanent import: a normal import declaration, with duty and import VAT as applicable.

Is there customs on deliveries to Ireland?

No. The Republic of Ireland is in the EU, so it is an intra-community delivery — the same procedure as shipping to Belgium or the Netherlands, zero-rated on a valid VAT number. Asking for a direct France–Ireland ferry rather than the UK land bridge keeps British customs out of it entirely.

Can I reclaim the import VAT on a UK delivery?

Import VAT is charged at the border and is generally recoverable if you are VAT-registered in the UK, through your normal return. The mechanics depend on your registration and whether you use postponed accounting — confirm it with your accountant or broker before the order.

What do you need from me for a UK shipment?

An EORI number, the name of the party acting as importer of record, and the delivery address with the build-up slot. Settle those at order stage rather than at dispatch: a missing importer of record is the most common reason a consignment stops moving.

How much extra lead time does the UK need?

Plan on confirming sizes about three weeks out rather than the week of the show. Standard sizes are in stock and we normally dispatch within 7 days of order confirmation; the customs step comes after dispatch, not instead of it. Ireland plans like any EU delivery.

Do you handle the customs clearance yourselves?

No — we are panel manufacturers, not customs agents. We supply the commercial documentation the declaration needs, correctly and on time. The commodity code, duty rate and importer of record are questions for your freight forwarder or customs broker.

A UK or Irish build-up to plan?

Send us the drawing, the quantities and the build-up slot: we will tell you the dispatch date and what the paperwork needs from your side. Written reply normally within 24 working hours.
