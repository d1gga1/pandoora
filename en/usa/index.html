What a honeycomb panel is — and why US exhibit builders use it

A honeycomb panel (also called a hollow-core or torsion-box panel) is a finished wall element: a frame in knot-free, finger-jointed spruce, a perforated paper honeycomb core and two MDF skins bonded under press. It is flat, rigid and a fraction of the weight of a solid MDF or plywood wall of the same size. It joins to the next panel with alignment strips and I·L·T·X steel connectors, and after the show it is stacked, crated and stored for the next build.

In a US show that weight matters twice. Material handling (drayage) is billed by weight — per hundredweight — by the general service contractor, both into and out of the hall, so every pound you do not ship is a cost you do not pay. And a lighter wall is a faster wall: fewer people to lift it, less time on the I&D crew's clock. The panels arrive raw (ready for paint, vinyl or fabric), white laminated, black laminated or lacquered in any RAL color.

Most US shows follow the IAEE display guidelines, which cap the back wall of an inline booth at 8 ft. A 2,500 mm stock panel is 8 ft 2 in — two inches too tall. Tell us the booth type and we cut to 2,440 mm, or to whatever the exhibitor manual allows. The same goes for widths: a 10 ft booth module is 3,048 mm, so three 1,016 mm (40 in) panels close it exactly. Custom sizes cost the same as stock sizes, from a single piece, up to 1,200 mm (47.2 in) wide and 6,000 mm (19 ft 8 in) long.

Fire rules: what to settle before the crate is closed

In the United States, booth construction rules are set by the local fire authority and published in each show's exhibitor manual. The common thread: construction materials must be flame retardant, and a certificate of flame retardancy should be kept at the booth so the fire marshal does not have to test material on site.

Our fire-rated panels carry the European classification B-s1,d0 under EN 13501-1 (and Italian Class 1), with the MDF supplier's certificate and our declaration of conformity on every delivery. That is a European test system, not a US test report. So we send the documents before the order ships, you or your general service contractor check them against the show's fire regulations, and if the fire marshal asks for something else we tell you honestly, at quote stage, what we can and cannot provide. More on the European side in our fire rating guide.

How a US order works

  1. Send the drawing. DWG, DXF, a dimensioned PDF, SketchUp, Rhino or STEP — or a hand sketch. We answer the same day, Italian time, with feasibility and a written quote in English; the Incoterm (who pays freight, duty and clearance) is written into it.
  2. Production. Standard sizes ship from stock in 2–3 working days; custom sizes in 7 days from order confirmation; RAL lacquered or special finishes in 10–12 days.
  3. Packing for export. Panels are numbered to your drawing, wrapped and crated or palletized for ocean or air freight, with a packing list per crate.
  4. Freight. Ocean freight when the calendar allows; air freight when the show will not wait. Your forwarder or ours delivers to your shop, the advance warehouse or the show site within the target move-in window.
  5. Customs. Your customs broker files the entry; we provide the commercial invoice, packing list and material data. Details on shipping panels to the USA.

Frequently asked questions

Do you really ship honeycomb panels from Italy to the United States?

Yes. We have already built for US shows — the Gruppo Romani booth at Coverings 2026 at the Las Vegas Convention Center stood on our panels — and we pack every export order in numbered crates or pallets with a packing list per piece.

Can you make panels in US sizes?

Yes. Every panel is cut to size at the same price as a stock panel, from a single piece: 8 ft inline walls, 40 in widths for a 10 ft module, or any size on your drawing, up to 47.2 in wide and 19 ft 8 in long.

Why buy booth walls from Italy instead of building them locally?

Because a finished, flat, light panel saves shop time before the show, drayage cost at the show (billed by weight) and storage space after it, and it can be reused show after show. For a first stock that will stay in the US, ocean freight keeps the landed cost reasonable.

Are your panels accepted by US fire marshals?

Our fire-rated panels are classified B-s1,d0 under the European standard EN 13501-1. US rules are set per venue and per show, so we send the documentation before shipping for you or your general service contractor to check, and tell you at quote stage if a requirement falls outside what we can document.

How fast can you deliver to a US show?

Production takes 2–3 working days from stock, 7 days for custom sizes and 10–12 days for RAL lacquered finishes. Freight adds the transit time: weeks by ocean, days by air. Send us the move-in date and we plan backwards from it.

Do you install the booth?

No — installation is done by your exhibit house or the I&D contractor on site. We number every piece to the drawing and supply the connectors and assembly instructions so the crew builds it in order.
