Shell scheme vs space only: which one to book

What each one actually gives you

Every UK show floor plan divides into two kinds of space, and the choice is made when you book — often months before anyone thinks about walls.

How to choose, honestly

We supply the walls for space-only builds. We will still say plainly when shell scheme is the better call — it usually is, in three situations.

Take shell scheme if the stand is small and the show is one-off. On 6 or 9 m², at one show, the shell scheme package with carpet and lighting included is hard to beat on cost and impossible to beat on effort. Put the budget into the graphics instead.

Take shell scheme if nobody on your side will be on site. It is up when you arrive. A space-only build needs somebody present who can make decisions during the build-up.

Take shell scheme if this is your first show in the UK. Learn the venue, the traffic flow and the build-up rules on somebody else's structure, then go space only next time knowing what you actually need.

Space only earns its cost when at least one of these is true:

  • You need height. The single most common reason. Shell scheme stops around 2.4 m; visibility across a hall starts at 3 m. We build walls up to 6,000 mm in one piece, which avoids the horizontal joint at mid-height that shows in every photograph.
  • The stand has to look like you, not like the show. Shell scheme gives everyone the same silhouette. A RAL-lacquered wall in your brand colour, a curve, a seamless run — those have to be made.
  • You are exhibiting more than once. This is where the arithmetic turns, and it turns faster than people expect — the full calculation is here.
  • The product is big, heavy, or has to hang on the wall. Shell scheme infill panels are not built to carry load. Ours are, and the reinforced version exists precisely for screens and brackets.

The option most exhibitors miss: building inside a shell scheme

It is not a binary. You can book shell scheme space and build your own structure inside it — and at many shows you can go up to about 4 m doing so, well above the shell scheme's own height.

This is the most under-used trick on the UK circuit. You keep the cheap package rate with its carpet and power, and you put a real wall in front of the organiser's panels instead of sticking graphics onto fabric. The result photographs like a space-only stand at a shell scheme floor rate.

Two things to check before you rely on it: the organiser's rules on building within shell scheme — they vary by show and are in the exhibitor manual — and whether you have to keep the fascia board, since some shows require it. Both are one email to the organiser.

What we supply for that job is the same as for a full space-only build: walls made to the drawing, dry-assembled with connectors and alignment rails, so it goes up in a build-up slot and comes down again intact.

Frequently asked questions

What is a shell scheme stand?

A modular stand provided by the show organiser: aluminium poles with infill panels, typically 1 m wide and 2.4 m tall, plus a fascia board with your company name and usually carpet, lighting and a power socket. It is up when you arrive; you add graphics.

What does space only mean at an exhibition?

You are allocated an empty floor area marked out with tape, and nothing else. You build the stand yourself or appoint a contractor, and you supply flooring, lighting and power. It costs a similar rate per square metre, with the build as a separate cost on top.

Which is cheaper, shell scheme or space only?

The floor rate is usually similar, but shell scheme works out cheaper overall for a single small stand because carpet, lighting and power are in the package. Space only becomes the better value once you need height, a distinctive design, load-bearing walls, or you are exhibiting more than once.

Can you build your own stand inside a shell scheme?

At many shows, yes — and often up to around 4 m, well above the shell scheme's own height. You keep the package rate and put a proper wall in front of the organiser's panels. Check the organiser's rules on building within shell scheme and whether the fascia board must be retained; both are in the exhibitor manual.

How tall can a space-only stand wall be?

Up to the hall limit, which is normally well above shell scheme height. We manufacture up to 6,000 mm in one piece, which removes the horizontal joint at mid-height. Above 3,000 mm plan for corner returns: stability comes from the connection system, not from the panel alone.

Do you build the stand as well as supply the panels?

No. We are the panel manufacturer: we make the walls, doors, platforms and connectors to your drawing and deliver them. We do not design or build stands and we do not appear on your stand — our customers are contractors, shopfitters, joiners and design studios.
