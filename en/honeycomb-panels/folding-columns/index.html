The volume problem in transport

Columns give a stand its vertical: totems, corners, wayfinding, supports for signage. The problem is that a column is by definition a volume: it takes up space in the store all year and space in the van at every show — and transport is charged on volume before weight.

The folding columns resolve that contradiction: they open in position and fold flat for transport. At the show they are a full architectural volume; in the van they are flat panels. On a stand that travels to several shows in a season, the saving on transport and storage adds up edition after edition — and on a show abroad it pays for itself quickly.

The mirrored version

The folding columns are also available mirrored, and that is the combination that makes them most recognisable: a mirrored column multiplies the hall light and the perceived depth of the stand without taking another square metre.

The same advice applies as on the mirror panels page: the surface shows marks, so plan a daily clean, and use the mirror as an accent rather than as blanket cladding.

Frequently asked questions

How does the folding mechanism work?

The column is opened into position during the build and folded flat for transport and storage. A full volume at the show, the footprint of flat panels in the van.

Is a mirrored version available?

Yes, the mirrored version is in the range: it multiplies the light and the perceived depth of the stand.

Can they be made bespoke?

Yes, customisable in size, shape and finish, including single pieces to drawing or specification.
